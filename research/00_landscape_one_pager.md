# Open-source orbit visualization and trajectory optimization, a landscape summary

Prepared by Surya Thirukonda for Prof. Sivakumar Rathinam. Snapshot date September 1, 2026. Full evidence in the six track documents and the gap analysis in this folder.

## What exists today

**Earth-orbit viewers (polished, no optimization).** satvis (MIT, TypeScript, CesiumJS, satellite.js, Vue) is the architecture and polish bar: live CelesTrak catalogs, SGP4 in a web worker on an epoch-anchored sample grid, batched primitives that hold roughly 5,000 to 12,000 satellites above 70 fps, seven per-satellite components (point, label, orbit, orbit track, ground track, sensor cone, 3D model), ground stations with pass prediction, and shareable URL state. KeepTrack.space (AGPL-3.0, custom WebGL2) is the performance ceiling at 50,000 or more objects, but its license blocks reuse in an MIT project. Stuff in Space is dead at its original site; an MIT fork survives. Gpredict and PREDICT (GPL) carry the ham-radio pass-prediction lineage that satvis webified.

**Interplanetary and mission-design tools (powerful, weak or absent viewers).** pykep and pygmo (ESA, MPL-2.0, active) give Lambert, multiple gravity assist, low-thrust and global optimization with static matplotlib plots only. GMAT (NASA, Apache-2.0, active) couples single-objective targeting and NLP optimization to a dated OpenGL 3D view. NASA EMTG (NOSA license) is the optimizer ceiling with no interactive view. poliastro was archived in October 2023 and its fork hapsira is dormant. spacekit.js (MIT) renders the solar system in Three.js but is stuck on an old WebGL1 three.js and fixed 2018 planetary elements. NASA Eyes on the Solar System is the visual bar and is closed source. STK, FreeFlyer and Copernicus are closed.

**Recent hobbyist tools.** Four single-author repositories created in 2026 (chipi/orrery, kevinkicho/k-solar-system-navigator, abc000cool/porkchop-mission-planner, tamiralmas/Orbital-Simulation) already couple a single-objective Lambert porkchop grid to an interactive 3D solar-system view. They have 0 to 3 stars, no institutional backing, no validation against oracles, and three of the four disclose heavy AI-assisted authorship. A game-branded planner, nmisyats/KSP-MGA-Planner (GPL-3.0), is the most mature coupled optimizer found.

## The gap, stated carefully

No open-source project, mature or not, couples an exact multi-objective (EMOA*-class, Pareto-front) optimizer to an interactive 3D view. No mature, institutionally backed open-source tool couples any trajectory optimizer to a browser-based interactive 3D scene. The only historical near-match, ESA's Space Trajectory Analysis tool, paired NSGA-II with a Celestia view and has been abandoned since 2013. The 2025 survey of multi-objective search names no space or trajectory application at all, which is a benchmark gap this project can fill.

## What was learned (verified, not recited)

- The six classical elements to position pipeline was implemented and checked against Curtis Example 4.7 to floating-point precision. The master document's written rotation formula had a sign error; the code was already correct.
- TLE mean elements are only valid inside SGP4. Feeding the ISS TLE to a two-body propagator is 11.7 km wrong at epoch and roughly 500 km wrong after one day. SGP4 outputs TEME; treating TEME as J2000 is a silent error of about 41 km at ISS altitude and about 260 km at GEO in 2026. The correct chain is TEME, GMST rotation, Earth-fixed, geodetic, which is exactly what satvis does in its worker.
- CelesTrak now enforces a 2-hour cadence with HTTP 403 on repeat downloads, and catalog numbers passed 100000 in July 2026, so new code should ingest OMM JSON, not TLE text.
- A porkchop for Earth to Mars over 2026 to 2029 was computed from JPL DE421 ephemerides and Izzo's Lambert solver. The best 2026 window departs October 29, 2026 with C3 of 9.3 km^2/s^2 and a v-infinity sum of 5.6 km/s, matching the expected 5.6 to 6 km/s band, with the expected 780-day recurrence.
- Weighted-sum search provably misses unsupported Pareto points; a three-point counterexample was reproduced numerically. In EMOA*, the balanced-tree frontier gives an asymptotic win at exactly three objectives, and the AIJ 2025 experiments favor a sorted-array variant. The reference C++ implementation, public_emoa, has no license file and must stay an optional plugin.
- The Georgia Tech space-logistics school solves campaign logistics as network-flow LP and MILP problems and, since 2026, has exact and evolutionary multi-objective variants. A daily-resolution Earth-Moon-Mars network over two years has roughly 9,000 time-expanded nodes and 40,000 arcs, so the Phase C graph builder must be event-driven, as that school's own 16-node, 598-arc network shows.

## Proposed v1 and sequencing

Phase 0 (weekend): the lecture as code. Elements to position, Kepler's equation, one animated orbit around a sphere, sliders for a, e, i, RAAN and argument of periapsis. Phase A: the ISS from a live OMM record on a token-free CesiumJS globe with orbit line, ground track, live state and an element panel that mirrors the lecture. Differentiators over satvis, in order of value per effort: an element-sandbox teaching mode (nothing in the genre lets a user edit elements and watch the orbit deform live), a porkchop linked to a 3D arc, and the Pareto route explorer that is the research contribution. Stack: CesiumJS behind a renderer interface for Earth, a separate Three.js heliocentric scene later, pykep or an in-house Izzo port for Lambert, and public_emoa or a TypeScript port for exact multi-objective search. Publication path supported by the evidence: a tool paper (SoCS or JOSS) plus a benchmark instance set, before any methods paper.

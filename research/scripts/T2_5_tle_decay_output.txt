4. J2 secular rates for the ISS orbit (what SGP4 models and two-body Kepler cannot)
====================================================================================================
nodal regression  dRAAN/dt = -3/2 n J2 (Re/p)^2 cos i        = -4.9481 deg/day
apsidal rotation  dargp/dt = 3/4 n J2 (Re/p)^2 (5cos^2 i - 1) = +3.6927 deg/day
mean-anomaly rate change from J2                              = +0.6213 deg/day
=> cross-track displacement from one day of ignored node regression: a*sin(i)*|dRAAN| = 460.3 km/day (max, 90 deg from the node)
=> Kepler period from TLE n: 92.966 min; J2 argument-of-latitude period n+argp_dot+M_dot: 92.894 min (diff -4.31 s/rev)
=> ground-track westward shift per orbit (Earth rotation + node regression): 23.61 deg

5. Analytic model: catalog-TLE along-track error growth from drag (ballistic coefficient error f)
====================================================================================================
Model: if the fitted drag is wrong by a fraction f, mean anomaly error dM(t) = 1/2 f ndot t^2 (rev),
       along-track error ds = 2 pi a dM.  Uses this TLE's ndot = 7.820e-05 rev/day^2 and a = 6797.9 km.
     f     0.5 d       1 d       2 d       3 d       7 d      14 d

  0.10      0.04      0.17      0.67      1.50      8.18     32.73  km
  0.20      0.08      0.33      1.34      3.01     16.37     65.47  km
  0.50      0.21      0.84      3.34      7.52     40.92    163.67  km
  1.00      0.42      1.67      6.68     15.03     81.83    327.33  km
Note: during a geomagnetic storm the density can double for a day (f ~ 1); post-storm TLEs are the worst.
Also note along-track error from a mean-motion error dn (rev/day): ds = 2 pi a dn t; the TLE prints n to 1e-8 rev/day,
so quantisation alone gives 0.4 m/day -- negligible; the real error is in the fitted n and drag.

6. Pixel scale on a Cesium globe and time-to-visibility for each orbit class
====================================================================================================

Cesium form: PerspectiveFrustum fov default = pi/3 (60 deg) on the wider axis; at slant distance D to the
point under the cursor, km/px ~= 2 D tan(fov/2) / W_px.  Whole Earth in 1000 px at fov 60 deg needs the camera at
  R/sin(30 deg) = 2R = 12742 km from Earth's center (6371 km altitude); sub-camera point scale = 7.36 km/px, limb scale ~ 12.74 km/px.
  Earth disk fills 1000 px (whole-globe view)                  12.742 km/px
  Earth disk fills 2000 px (4K-ish or zoomed a little)          6.371 km/px
  2000 km region across 1000 px (continent)                     2.000 km/px
  500 km region across 1000 px (regional/pass view)             0.500 km/px
